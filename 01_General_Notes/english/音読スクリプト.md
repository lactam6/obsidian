## 1. Small Talk (日常会話)

A: How's it going?

B: I'm pretty good. Actually, I'm a little sleepy because I stayed up late. How about you?

A: I'm great! What's new?

B: Last weekend I went to a concert. It was amazing.

A: Sounds great! I stayed home to study, so I'm a bit jealous.

B: Oh, that's too bad. You should come with us next time.

## 2. Classroom English (教室での英語)

Student: Excuse me? What does "procrastinate" mean?

Teacher: It means to delay or postpone something.

Student: I'm sorry, I don't understand. Could you please say that again?

Teacher: Sure. It means waiting until the last minute to do your work.

Student: Oh, I see. How do you spell it?

Teacher: P-R-O-C-R-A-S-T-I-N-A-T-E.

Student: Thank you. How do you say "shime-kiri" in English?

Teacher: It's "deadline."

## 3. Directly Expressing Opinions (直接的な意見)

A: What do you think about working from home?

B: In my opinion, it's much more productive. As I see it, you save a lot of time on commuting.

A: I really think social interaction is important, though.

B: The point is, we can still talk through video calls. As far as I'm concerned, results matter more than where you sit.

## 4. Indirectly Expressing Opinions (間接的な意見)

A: I'd like to point out that the city park is getting very crowded lately.

B: Don't you agree that we need more green spaces in the center?

A: I sometimes think that people prefer malls over parks these days.

B: Wouldn't you say that fresh air is better for children, though?

## 5. Agreeing (賛成)

A: I think the new cafeteria food is much better than before.

B: Exactly! I completely agree with you.

A: And the prices are very reasonable, too.

B: That's a good point. I think so too. I couldn't agree more.

## 6. Indirect Disagreeing (控えめな反対)

A: Everyone should learn three languages in high school.

B: Yes, that's quite true, but it might be too much pressure for students.

A: But it helps with global communication.

B: I see what you mean, but I'm not sure if everyone has the talent for languages.

## 7. More Direct Disagreeing (はっきりした反対)

A: Video games are a complete waste of time.

B: I don't think so. They can improve problem-solving skills.

A: They just make people violent.

B: You can't say that. That's not the point. Many games are educational and creative.

## 8. Close ones Disagreeing (親しい間での反対)

A: I'm going to quit my job and become a professional YouTuber tomorrow.

B: Are you kidding? You don't even have a camera!

A: I can just use my phone.

B: No way. You've got to be kidding. That's a huge risk! Don't make me laugh.

## 9. Making Suggestions (提案)

A: I'm always tired these days. What should I do?

B: I think it might be a good idea to go to bed earlier.

A: I try, but I can't fall asleep.

B: Have you ever thought of exercising in the afternoon? You might want to try yoga.

A: That sounds interesting.

B: Why don't you join a trial lesson this weekend?

## 10. Asking Indirect Questions (間接的な質問)

A: I wonder if you could tell me where the library is?

B: It's just around the corner.

A: Could you tell me if it's open until 9 PM?

B: Yes, it is.

A: One more thing. I hope you don't mind my asking, but I'd like to know if they have a Wi-Fi password.

## 11. Asking for More Information (情報をさらに求める)

A: I'm planning a trip to Hokkaido.

B: Could you tell me some more about your itinerary?

A: I'm visiting Sapporo and Otaru.

B: I'd like to know more about the food you're planning to eat.

A: I want to try the fresh seafood and ramen.

B: Something else I was wondering about was how long you are staying there?
